Compose around the evidence gap.

A prompt revision may need only offline eval; a permission change may mainly need tests; an AI workflow may combine contract tests, output eval, and production validation. A shared harness should preserve the meaning of rule failures, weak quality, invalid runs, and insufficient evidence.

Method reference · golden set, robustness, A/B, and rollout patterns

These tools can support several approaches above. Choose one when it closes a specific evidence gap.

NameRole in the systemWhen to add it
Golden set · Reference casesCase asset for tests, evals, and benchmarks. Curated cases carry reviewed reference answers or expected judgments.Use for stable version comparisons, regression checks, or grader calibration. Version the set and keep independent held-out cases.
Robustness checksCoverage method: vary inputs, context, or operating conditions; measure both rule compliance and changes in quality.Use when missing information, noise, distribution shifts, or dependency failures can affect performance.
Load / stress testsOperational method: measure capacity, latency, degradation, and recovery under traffic or resource pressure.Use to establish supported scale and what happens when capacity is exceeded.
Red-team / blue-team exercisesAdversarial assurance: challenge safety and permission boundaries, then validate defenses and response.Use for abuse paths, unauthorized actions, and other high-risk behavior. Record findings as repeatable cases where possible.
A/B and A/AExperiment designs: A/B compares different treatments; A/A uses identical treatments to check assignment and measurement.Use A/B for causal effect estimates; use A/A when validating new or materially changed experiment infrastructure.
Shadow and canaryRelease patterns: shadow runs a candidate alongside live traffic without applying its outputs; canary exposes a small controlled share to the new version.Use for real-environment evidence with controlled rollout risk. Isolate shadow side effects; define monitoring and rollback gates for canaries.

What should the agent send for human review?

Each request should include the actual output, relevant input, criterion, agent assessment, and one specific question. Prioritize borderline scores, conflicting judgments, unfamiliar failures, and serious risks; sample some passes to check for missed problems.

Implementation reference · contracts, runner, graders, and run records

The harness connects cases, execution, graders, and records. Gates define whether the evidence is sufficient to proceed.

PartPurposeWhat to specify
1 · Eval contractAgree how evidence will support a decision.Decision, evaluation unit, criteria, thresholds, hard limits, and actions for pass, fail, or insufficient evidence.

Eval matrix

Connect coverage, criteria, and scoring

An eval matrix maps cases to applicable criteria. Graders supply the judgments or measurements. Manage cases, criteria, and graders separately so each can be reused and audited.

Scenario axes · Where to measure

The same feature may behave differently across users, inputs, permissions, and operating conditions. Use axes to choose cases systematically.

Criteria · What counts as good

Define invariants, quality dimensions, scoring anchors, thresholds, operating limits, and capabilities to protect.

Graders · How to judge

Use code for exact rules and validated model or human judgment for contextual quality. Keep evidence and uncertainty with the score.

Scenario axes · selection questions and examples

Select the axes that materially change performance or risk. Input completeness and context consistency are separate axes: a complete brief can still contain conflicting facts.

Coverage dimensionWhat to considerExamples
User and domainDo requirements change across users or domains?New and experienced users; different professional domains.
Goal and lifecycleWhat is the task, and where is the user in it?First creation, editing existing content, retry, or recovery.
Input richnessHow much information is available, and how should gaps be handled?Sufficient information, missing key fields, or a short description.
ContextAre sources consistent, relevant, and current?Consistent sources, conflicting facts, or stale information.
Risk and authorityWhat are the consequences of error, and what actions are allowed?Read-only advice, editing access, or approval required before execution.
Tool or provider stateHow does the feature behave when dependencies change?Normal, timeout, rate limit, or partial results.
Language and scaleDo language, length, or workload change performance?Multiple languages, long context, or batch tasks.

Prioritize frequent tasks, high-risk cases, and known failures. Add important combinations, such as sparse input plus authority to execute. Choose combinations by risk rather than enumerating every permutation.

Dataset partitions · development, regression, calibration, and held-out

Separate cases by their purpose and record any overlap. Repeatedly tuning against the final assessment cases makes the result less informative about unseen work.

PartitionUse
Development setTune the implementation, prompts, and rubric.
Regression setCheck that known capabilities and previous fixes still hold.
Calibration setCheck grader reliability against reviewed judgments.
Held-out setAssess performance on cases not used during tuning.
Grader validity · check scoring before using it for decisions

A grader can reward a fluent but incorrect answer. Check it on strong, weak, borderline, and serious-failure cases before using its scores for release decisions.

  • Provide the source facts and context needed to judge each criterion.
  • Define concrete scoring anchors; keep reasons tied to observable evidence.
  • Compare with qualified human ratings; inspect disagreement, missed serious failures, and false alarms.
  • Blind candidate identity where practical to reduce preference bias.
  • Version the model, instructions, rubric, context, and parser. Revalidate after material changes and define how disputes are adjudicated.
Compare alternatives

Compare against what users would actually use

A baseline is the reference option. A benchmark is the comparison process. First identify a credible alternative, then give each option a fair task and consistent scoring.

A · Current approach

Observe how a user completes the task today, including preparation and editing effort.

B · Same information

Give a capable alternative the same approved source material. This helps identify the value of the specialized workflow.

C · Your feature

Run the real feature from input to final result and account for the user’s remaining work.

Decision gate · agree what improvement is worth adopting

Choose comparison arms for your question. Weigh quality, user effort, cost, latency, and uncertainty against pre-agreed conditions.

  • Required safety and operating limits pass.
  • Protected capabilities stay within agreed non-regression limits.
  • The improvement is large and reliable enough to justify adoption.
  • Cost and user effort remain acceptable; otherwise iterate or retain the current option.
How to keep a comparison fair
  • Confirm the alternative’s current capability and version.
  • Keep the task, source material, deliverable, and scoring comparable. Document any deliberate differences.
  • Repeat variable runs and report the spread as well as the average.
  • Include preparation, retries, editing, and review effort in the comparison.
After release

Use real failures to improve the next check

Offline evals use a controlled case collection. Online evals inspect actual use. Connecting the two helps you catch both familiar regressions and new problems.

Offline: repeat known cases

Compare versions and safely reproduce failures with fixed inputs, without involving live users.

⇄

Online: learn from real use

Monitor errors and response time; sample outputs for quality. Turn confirmed new failures into reusable offline cases.

When do you need product analytics or an experiment?

Analytics shows whether people start, finish, or return. If completion improves after a release, other changes may also explain it. A controlled experiment helps estimate the effect of the specific change.

Implementation details

Build a trusted path, then expand coverage

The agent executes the approved work and analyzes results. Each phase produces evidence for the next; people retain decisions that require product judgment, risk acceptance, or execution authority.

1 · Define the problem and scope

Identify the decision, uncertainty, and evaluation unit.

Output: Verified gaps, a validation plan, and decision gates.

Actions and checkpoints
  • Inspect the real user path, code, contracts, existing evidence, environment, and authority. Mark anything not verified.
  • Choose tests, evals, and extensions around material risks; write the eval contract and agree scope before implementation.
  • When comparison is needed, research and select a credible baseline at this stage.

2 · Build a trustworthy execution path

Validate the cases and scoring, then connect one complete path.

Output: A repeatable run with traceable evidence.

Actions and checkpoints
  • Prepare versioned cases and dataset partitions; check data availability, grader context, human agreement, and comparison fairness.
  • Within the approved scope, connect stage contracts, runner, graders, replay, and run manifests into a narrow end-to-end slice.
  • Make permissions, budgets, side effects, timeouts, and invalid-run handling explicit.

3 · Run, analyze, and decide

Have the agent explain results and prepare focused human review.

Output: Result analysis, a review queue, and an adoption or improvement decision.

Actions and checkpoints
  • Run the selected checks and comparisons; preserve raw results and locate the earliest failing boundary.
  • Analyze patterns, uncertainty, protected regressions, and total user effort. Distinguish invalid runs from product failures.
  • Escalate hard-limit failures, borderline results, grader conflicts, new failure types, and decisions requiring human authority. Include evidence and a specific question; sample passes too.

4 · Validate in production and iterate

Connect live evidence to the next round of checks.

Output: Operational actions, regression cases, and the next validation priorities.

Actions and checkpoints
  • Use approved monitoring, online evals, rollout patterns, or experiments according to risk and decision needs.
  • Turn confirmed failures, distribution changes, and human judgments into governed updates to cases and graders.
  • Link decisions and follow-up actions to the evidence; repeat the relevant checks after changes.
