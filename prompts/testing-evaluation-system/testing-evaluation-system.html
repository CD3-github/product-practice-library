Evidence to decision

Testing & Evaluation System

Choose the evidence that matches the decision, automate the analysis, and send people only the judgments that require them.

Deterministic + probabilisticStage + E2EOffline + online
Every feature needs an evidence strategy. Tests, evals, benchmarks, production monitoring, experiments, and human review can be combined in different proportions.
Choose evidence

Start with the decision you need to make

Select the smallest trustworthy combination. Set the unit where uncertainty exists: function, stage, output, task, session, workflow, feature, release, or cohort.

Deterministic tests

Does behavior meet explicit contracts?

Use for

Schema, state, calculation, permission, side effect, recovery, and exact performance limits.

How it works

known input → exact assertion → localized pass / fail

Decision output

Correctness gate and first failing boundary.

Evals

How well does the system perform?

Use for

Quality, usefulness, grounding, task success, safety, cost, latency, reliability, or any defined criterion.

How it works

cases × criteria → code + heuristic + model + human graders → scorecard

Decision output

Strengths, weaknesses, uncertainty, and thresholds.

Benchmark

Which alternative performs better?

Use for

Versions, models, prompts, workflows, products, or the user's current alternative.

How it works

baseline research → fair arms → shared tests + evals + operating measures → delta

Decision output

Comparative value and trade-offs.

Production validation

What happens with real use?

Use for

Live quality, drift, incidents, acceptance, edits, adoption, product analytics, and unexpected behavior.

How it works

traces + events + online evals + monitoring + feedback → observed signals

Decision output

Operational response, product hypothesis, or a new offline case.

Controlled experiments

Did the intervention cause a meaningful change?

Use for

A/B or other defensible causal designs when traffic, ethics, instrumentation, and decision value justify them.

How it works

hypothesis + randomized exposure + control / treatment + guardrails → causal estimate

Decision output

Experiment-bounded causal impact and a ship, iterate, or stop decision.

How to combine tests and evals without forcing a false choice
  • Test-heavy: deterministic product behavior with exact contracts and limited judgment.
  • Eval-led: open-ended AI behavior measured by a mix of exact metrics, code graders, model judges, and human review.
  • One shared harness: valid when it preserves result type, provenance, and failure semantics; separate infrastructure is not required merely for taxonomy.
  • Cross-cutting measures: cost, latency, reliability, retries, and human effort can appear in tests, evals, benchmarks, experiments, and production validation.
Method selector: golden sets, A/B tests, red teams, robustness, and release patterns
Method or assetWhat it isChoose it when
Golden setCurated, versioned case asset—not a separate test layer.You need regression, judge calibration, or stable comparison cases.

Human + agent collaboration

The agent analyzes the run; people make the consequential decisions

The same evidence is translated into three decision perspectives. These are not prescribed job titles; one person may cover several.

Product

  • What result did we get?
  • Which user or product goal does it represent?
  • What decision does it support?
  • What is the next product lever?

Engineering

  • Is the result valid and reproducible?
  • Where did behavior first diverge?
  • Is the defect in product, data, harness, grader, or environment?
  • What should be diagnosed or repaired?

Quality & operations

  • Does it reflect real domain and workflow conditions?
  • What is unsafe, costly, unsupported, or ambiguous?
  • What needs human acceptance or adjudication?
  • Is the system operationally ready?
1 · Automated runAuthorized tests, evals, benchmark arms, and operational measures.
2 · Agent analysisValidate the ruler, localize failure, cluster patterns, assess uncertainty.
3 · TriageNo action · agent follow-up · human review · blocked/unverified.
4 · Human decisionReview only prioritized evidence and sampled passes where risk warrants it.
5 · FeedbackConfirmed findings become labels, cases, rubric changes, or regression checks.
Escalate: hard-cap or high-risk cases, borderline thresholds, grader disagreement, novel failure clusters, protected regressions, suspicious passes, and product/domain decisions automation cannot legitimately make.

Eval matrix

Cases define where to measure; criteria and graders define what and how

An eval can combine deterministic, operational, semantic, model-judged, and human-judged evidence. Keeping cases, criteria, and graders separate makes each reusable and auditable.

Scenario axes

Meaningful variation

User/domain, goal, input richness, lifecycle, risk, authority, context, tool/provider state, language, and scale.

Partitions

Prevent overfitting

Development, regression, judge calibration, and held-out sets have different jobs and must not be silently reused.

Criteria

Define performance first

Exact invariants, quality dimensions, operating limits, anchors, thresholds, hard caps, and protected strengths.

Grader validity

Test the ruler

Use the simplest valid grader; verify context, blinding, human agreement, error modes, versioning, and adjudication.

What the model judge must prove before it can become a gate
  • It receives enough producer context to distinguish grounded details from fabrication.
  • Its scale has concrete anchors and requires evidence-based reasons.
  • It is compared with qualified human ratings across weak, borderline, strong, and hard-cap cases.
  • Agreement, rank correlation, dimension errors, and hard-cap false positives/negatives are understood.
  • Its model, prompt, rubric, context packet, and parser are versioned and revalidated after material change.
Benchmark design

Research the baseline before running the benchmark

A baseline is the comparator. A benchmark is the controlled procedure that applies shared measures to baseline and candidate arms.

Observe alternativesWhat would the user actually do today?
Research the baselineCapability, inputs, context, effort, cost, and current version.
Freeze fair armsSame task, approved context, deliverable, and accounting rules.
Apply shared measuresTests, eval dimensions, repeats, cost, latency, and human effort.
CompareDelta, variance, protected regressions, and trade-offs.

  • Natural preparation effort

  • Same deliverable requirement

Production evidence

Close the offline–online loop

Current AI engineering practice usually distinguishes offline evals from online evals and production monitoring. Use experiments when the decision requires causal impact rather than observation.

Offline eval

Curated or historical cases before release. Compare versions, catch regressions, calibrate graders, and reproduce failures safely.

⇄

Online eval + production monitoring

Sample live traces, track reliability and quality, detect drift and incidents, then turn confirmed failures into new offline cases and regression checks.

When monitoring is not enough

Observed adoption, conversion, or business movement may correlate with a release without being caused by it. Use a controlled experiment or another defensible causal design when the decision requires attribution.

Adjacent practice

Quality evidence and product evidence meet in production

This page owns correctness, quality, readiness, and the connection to real use. A sibling Product Analytics & Experimentation practice should own the deeper workflow for behavioral measurement and causal product decisions.

Testing & Eval owns

Tests, offline and online evals, benchmarks, quality/reliability monitoring, grader validation, regressions, risk gates, and evidence needed for release readiness.

Analytics & Experiments owns

Event and exposure contracts, adoption, funnels, cohorts, retention, observed outcomes, experiment design, causal inference, and product-impact decisions.

Offline eval quality→Production analytics + online quality→Controlled experiment when causality matters
Shared foundation: identity and cohort definitions, event and exposure contracts, variant and release versions, trace IDs, quality/safety/cost guardrails, privacy rules, and an append-only decision log. A good result in one layer does not silently prove another.
