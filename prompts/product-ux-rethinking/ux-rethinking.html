Establish the real baseline

Read the implemented capability before judging the experience. Product documents describe intent; code, contracts, fixtures, and observed behavior establish different parts of the current state.

  1. Capability evidenceRead the real paths and limits.
  2. User jobDefine the outcome and decisions.
  3. ResponsibilityMove resolvable work into the system.
  4. Target experienceRebuild objects, flow, and content.
SourceWhat it establishesBoundary
DocumentsIntended behavior, scope, and decisions.Check approval and freshness; intent does not establish execution.
Code and contractsImplemented paths, states, validations, and dependencies.Verify registration, reachability, and real composition.
Fixtures and runtimeFixtures illustrate states; observed runs show behavior for a scenario.Label demo-only, partial, live-verified, and unknown capability.
Baseline inspection checklist
  • Record branch, commit, worktree, dirty files, routes, components, service calls, and provider boundaries.
  • Walk the entry-to-result path, including permission, loading, empty, stale, partial, and returning states.
  • Identify actions that really execute and claims that only exist in UI or fixtures.

Diagnose friction and responsibility

Look for work that the interface asks users to do because the implementation has not resolved it. Keep human decisions when their consequences require judgment or authority.

FrictionQuestionPotential response
ComprehensionCan people tell what matters and what to do next?Organize around the task and primary decision.
Input and choiceAre users repeating known information or selecting internal mechanisms?Resolve trusted context and safe defaults; expose consequential choices.
Trust and executionAre facts, advice, authority, and actual results distinguishable?Show source, readiness, action consequences, and verified state.
RecoveryCan people resume after partial success, timeout, or interruption?Preserve progress, reconcile state, and make safe next actions clear.
Quality protection

For every removed input or step, explain how trusted context, validated defaults, server logic, or an explicit gap replaces it. Verify that reduced effort preserves the required result.

Choose the depth of change

Compare three levels of intervention against product maturity and the cost of keeping the current model. Recommend one direction with evidence.

DirectionWhat changesBest fit
Conservative integrationCorrect major friction, truth, and authority issues within a mostly stable structure.The mental model works and specific defects dominate.
Structural rethinkRework objects, information architecture, state, and primary flow.Useful capabilities are trapped in fragmented or implementation-led workflows.
User-first reimaginationRebuild the experience from the user job and a new responsibility model.The existing product model creates unnecessary work or wrong expectations.
Compare variations using the same scenario

Use the same user, data, edge state, quality bar, and authority boundary. Compare time to understand, input burden, decision count, trust, recovery, migration cost, and risks. Draw side-by-side mockups only when a genuine structural choice remains.

Give every information item a job

Retain the information needed for a good decision, express it at the right moment, and account for what replaces anything removed.

DispositionUse it whenCheck
KeepIt directly supports the current task or decision.Meaning, source, and default visibility.
RewriteThe information matters but its language or framing obscures its use.Outcome-led copy, correct units, and uncertainty.
Progressive disclosureExperts or exceptional cases need detail.Keep decision-critical facts visible; make detail discoverable.
MoveIt belongs to another object, stage, role, or destination.A clear destination and retrieval path.
RemoveIt is redundant, unsupported, unactionable, or internal-only.Dependent contracts and the replacement for any useful function.
AddTrust, readiness, decision quality, or recovery lacks necessary information.Owner, source, state, and ongoing maintenance.

Validate one structural slice

Separate UX/content changes from frontend, backend, and data-contract changes. Implement only the approved slice, then compare the real experience with the baseline.

Friction and output quality

Check comprehension, inputs, decisions, recovery effort, and whether the result remains accurate and useful.

Capability truth and safety

Verify actual responses, permissions, readiness, idempotency, partial outcomes, and recovery.

Migration contract
  • Proposal ID, selected direction, user outcome, scope, and preserved behavior.
  • Affected UI/content, frontend, backend/API, data sources, and migration dependencies.
  • Acceptance evidence, test/eval plan, rollout boundary, and rollback strategy.
  • Prioritize misleading claims and broken authority, then a complete path, then broader visual and advanced capability work.
