Start from the decision

Research is useful when its findings can change an action: whom to serve, which problem to prioritize, which alternative to compare, or which assumption to test.

  1. DecisionWhat action may change?
  2. UncertaintyWhat must we learn?
  3. EvidenceWhat can answer it?
  4. Next actionProceed, narrow, test, or pause.

Research brief

Name the user, task, decision, deadline, and current alternative. Turn opinions into questions that evidence can answer.

Assumption priority

Investigate assumptions with high consequence and weak evidence first. Set a time and cost boundary before collecting more material.

Example · turn a feature request into a research question

“Add an AI reply button” is a proposed solution. Ask where support staff spend effort today, which replies require judgment, and what prevents a draft from being sent. Those answers may support drafting, better source retrieval, or a review queue.

Choose the evidence approach

Choose methods for the uncertainty and population. Several methods can contribute to one decision, with different limits on what they establish.

QuestionApproachWhat to watch
How is the task actually done?Interviews, contextual observation, task artifacts, support cases.Understand context and workarounds; separate what people say from what they do.
What alternatives exist?Desk research, primary documentation, comparable product walkthroughs.Record capability, version, effort, and limits; verify material claims.
Where and how often does friction occur?Validated analytics, segmentation, appropriately sampled surveys.Define the denominator, window, sample, and missing populations.
Can this capability work?API contracts, provider documentation, approved spike or eval.Feasibility evidence supports a bounded capability claim; demand needs its own evidence.
Did the change cause improvement?Controlled experiments or a defensible causal design.Use the analytics and experiments practice for design and interpretation.
Planning participant research · sample, consent, and neutral questions
  • Recruit for the task and relevant differences in expertise, context, accessibility, and risk. Make selection bias explicit.
  • Ask about recent real behavior and artifacts before presenting a solution. Use open questions and neutral tasks.
  • Define consent, recording, access, redaction, and retention. Participant contact requires explicit authorization.
  • Choose sample size and additional rounds for the method, variation, and decision risk; report coverage limitations.

Turn sources into findings

Keep the observation separate from your interpretation. A team should be able to trace each recommendation back to what was actually learned.

  1. SourceDocument, interview, behavior, or runtime.
  2. ObservationWhat was said or seen.
  3. InterpretationPattern and alternative explanations.
  4. ImplicationWhat changes in the decision.
Finding recordPurposeExample field
Evidence and scopeLet others verify the claim and its population.Source ID, link, date/version, method, population.
Interpretation and uncertaintyKeep hypotheses reviewable instead of presenting them as observed facts.Pattern, contradictory cases, confidence, missing evidence.
Decision implicationMake research actionable.Affected assumption, recommended action, decision owner.
Quality checks · contradictions, freshness, and claims
  • Preserve conflicting reports and abandoned tasks. A minority pattern may identify a high-risk segment.
  • Count independent evidence, not copied claims repeated across websites. Check product version and regional availability.
  • A mention count is not prevalence without a sampling basis; an observed association does not establish causation.
  • Mark inaccessible sources and missing observations as unverified. AI-generated examples illustrate a method; they are not research participants.

Work with an agent

Automate source organization and first-pass synthesis, while preserving human responsibility for participant relationships and consequential choices.

Agent prepares and checks

Inspect context, choose a research route, collect authorized evidence, compare alternatives, surface contradictions, and draft an evidence-linked recommendation.

People decide and validate

Confirm the decision and acceptable risk, authorize participant work, review ambiguous interpretations, and decide what action the findings justify.

A useful research handoff

Return the recommendation, strongest findings, limitations, and the next question. Keep a linked evidence appendix for deeper review. Stop when the next decision is adequately supported or the approved budget is reached; retain unresolved uncertainty.
