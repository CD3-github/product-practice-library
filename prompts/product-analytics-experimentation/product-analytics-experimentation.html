Measurement foundation

Validate the ruler before interpreting movement

Every chart and experiment depends on shared definitions. Version the contracts, trace each metric to its source, and distinguish assignment from actual exposure.

01

Identity

User, account, workspace, device, merge, alias, and deletion rules.

02

Events

Meaning, trigger, owner, properties, authority, deduplication, and versions.

03

Metrics + cohorts

Population, numerator, denominator, window, unit, entry rule, and threshold.

04

Assignment + exposure

Eligibility, bucketing, variant, actual exposure, contamination, and fallback.

05

Decision record

Queries, versions, windows, exclusions, evidence, uncertainty, and action.

Validity gate: check completeness, duplicates, event order, identity joins, timezones, release coverage, denominator drift, assignment/exposure mismatch, test traffic, and query/dashboard agreement.

Controlled experimentation

Design the decision before starting the experiment

An A/B test compares a control and treatment under randomized assignment. It is useful only when exposure, metrics, power, risk, and the action after each outcome are defined before results are read.

Question

Hypothesis + mechanism

State what changes, why it should matter, and the smallest effect worth acting on.

Population

Eligibility + unit

Define who enters, what is randomized, what is analyzed, and how interference is handled.

Treatment

Assignment + exposure

Version the variants and distinguish assigned users from users who actually encounter the change.

Measurement

Primary + guardrails

Choose one decision-driving outcome plus diagnostics and quality, safety, cost, or business protections.

Validity

Power + integrity

Set baseline, MDE, sample and duration assumptions; check sample ratio, contamination, drift, and stopping.

Operations

Ramp + rollback

Define staged exposure, kill switch, owner, decision rule, follow-up window, and durable record.

Decision outcomesShip or expandIterate and retestKeep control or stopRepair measurement or collect evidence
A/A, A/B, canary, and shadow serve different purposes

A/A checks whether randomization, exposure, metrics, and statistical behavior are trustworthy when both arms are intentionally the same. A/B estimates the effect of a deliberate product difference. Canary limits how much real traffic receives a release while operational evidence is watched. Shadow runs a candidate on copied or mirrored traffic without making it the user-facing result. These methods can be combined, but they answer different questions.

Connected evidence

AI quality and product outcomes meet at the decision

A better offline score does not automatically improve user behavior, and higher usage does not prove output quality. Preserve each claim type, then join them with shared release, variant, and trace identifiers.

Offline eval

Pre-release quality, comparison, and regression evidence on curated or historical cases.

Online eval + monitoring

Live quality, safety, reliability, drift, incidents, cost, and latency.

Product analytics

Observed behavior, effort, adoption, conversion, retention, and association.

Useful shared signals: latency, cost, retries, human correction effort, acceptance, override, abandonment, task completion, and incident rate.
